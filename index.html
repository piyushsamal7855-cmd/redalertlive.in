URGENT: Inside the "Digital Arrest" Scam Terrorizing India – Do Not Pay!

Updated: May 6, 2026 | Reported by Red Alert Intelligence Team

Is a "CBI Officer" threatening you on a WhatsApp video call right now? Stop. Do not panic, and most importantly, do not transfer any money. You are being targeted by the "Digital Arrest" scam, the most dangerous cyber fraud sweeping India in 2026.

Millions of people across India are terrified and searching for answers as sophisticated criminal gangs impersonate law enforcement agencies (CBI, ED, Police, or even TRAI) to extort life savings. This is not a real arrest; it is a psychological trap.

How the "Digital Arrest" Scam Works

The scam usually begins with an automated call or SMS claiming your phone number, Aadhaar card, or bank account has been linked to illegal activities, such as money laundering or drug trafficking.

When you show fear, you are quickly transferred to a WhatsApp or Skype Video Call. On the other end, scammers sit in fake police station setups, complete with uniforms, badges, and official-looking paperwork to appear authentic.

Their Goal: To keep you on the video call for hours—sometimes days—claiming you are under "Digital Arrest." They forbid you from talking to family or disconnecting. They pressure you to transfer large sums of money into "safe government accounts" for verification, promising it will be refunded. It never is.

How to Verify a Fake CBI or Police Call Immediately

If you are targeted, your knowledge is your only defense. Law enforcement agencies do not operate over video calls. Here is how you can verify and defeat the scam instantly:

✅ Official Verification Checklist

  • Rule #1: No Video Arrests. Real CBI, Police, or ED officers never conduct investigation or make arrests over WhatsApp, Skype, or any video call. An arrest requires a physical warrant and physical presence.
  • Rule #2: No Money Demands. Government agencies will never ask you to transfer money to a private bank account for "verification." Any demand for money over a call is a scam.
  • Rule #3: Demand a Written Notice. A real investigation requires written notices under Section 41A of the CrPC. If they cannot produce a physical notice sent via official channels, it is a fraud.
  • Rule #4: Verify the Caller ID. Ignore "Truecaller" names, which can be faked. If they claim to be from a specific police station, disconnect and call that station’s official landline number found on the official government (.gov.in) website.

What to do if Targeted

If you receive such a call, disconnect immediately. Block the number. Do not engage in argument.

If you have already transferred money, time is critical. Immediately call India's official National Cyber Crime Helpline at 1930 to report the fraud and attempt to freeze the transaction. File a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in.

Stay vigilant. Share this alert to protect your family and friends.
